# A low-preparation-T no-go theorem for two-CNOT magic advantage

**Research date: 30 October 2026.** **Status:** proved for the exact two-qubit unitary Clifford+T model via complete signed Clifford-orbit enumeration and exact \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt2]\) witnesses, independently checked using complex 4×4 state vectors and explicit physical one-CNOT compilers. **Publication originality not established.**

## Central result

Let \(\mathcal S_{\le2T}\) contain **every pure two-qubit state** that can be prepared from **any pure two-qubit stabilizer state** by **at most two T gates and arbitrary two-qubit Clifford gates**, with no ancilla, measurement or postselection. The state's preparation is considered *prior to* the subsequent resource-conversion task, and its gate costs are *not* charged to that task.

For any supplied \(|\psi\rangle\in\mathcal S_{\le2T}\), permit **at most three additional standard one-qubit T gates, arbitrary single-qubit Cliffords, and at most one physical CNOT** (either orientation), and maximize final pure-state second stabilizer Rényi entropy \(M_2=-\log_2(R/4)\), where
\[
R(\psi)=\sum_{P\in\{I,X,Y,Z\}^{\otimes2}}\langle P\rangle_\psi^4.
\]

**Theorem (conditional low-T input no-go):**
\[
\boxed{
\forall\psi\in\mathcal S_{\le2T}:\quad
\min_{\le3\text{ additional }T,\ \le1\mathrm{CNOT}}R_{\rm out}(\psi)
=\frac74.
}
\]
The equality is the established **global** minimum fourth moment over all pure two-qubit states, so no additional CNOT can improve \(M_2\) for **any** input in this family under the stated budget.

**Corollary (necessary input complexity):** A pure two-qubit input exhibiting a **strict two-CNOT advantage** when maximizing final M2 with at most three additional T gates cannot be prepared from *any* stabilizer input with two or fewer T gates, even allowing arbitrary free Clifford routing during preparation. Thus such an input has minimum stabilizer-to-input exact T preparation count **at least three**. Our earlier engineered strict-gap witness requires four T gates to prepare, consistent with (but not implied by) this theorem.

The result is *not* a lower bound on the total T count of a globally optimal direct maximal-magic preparation: Liu–Low–Yin (2026) already construct maximum two-qubit magic directly from \(|++\rangle\) with three T and one CNOT.

## Why the family is finite and complete

Every pure stabilizer state is Clifford-related to \(|00\rangle\). Clifford conjugation maps a Pauli rotation \(T_P=e^{-i\pi P/8}\) to another Pauli rotation; \(T^\dagger\) can be represented using one \(T\) plus Clifford correction. Hence any Clifford+at-most-two-T input, modulo a **final Clifford**, is represented by **one of**
\[
|00\rangle,\qquad T_P|00\rangle,\qquad T_QT_P|00\rangle,
\]
where \(P,Q\) range over the 15 nonidentity Hermitian two-qubit Paulis. There are \(1+15+225=241\) such Pauli words.

Starting from each of these 241 states, exhaust the finite signed 2-qubit Clifford group generated by \(H_0,H_1,S_0,S_1,\mathrm{CNOT}_{0\to1}\) and deduplicate using all **16 signed Pauli expectation values**. This identifies **four distinct full Clifford orbits**:

| Preparation complexity | Orbit size |
|---|---:|
| Stabilizer inputs | 60 |
| Exactly one T (nonstabilizer) | 360 |
| Exactly two T, orbit A | 720 |
| Exactly two T, orbit B | 1,440 |
| **Total** | **2,580** |

Since one-qubit Cliffords are **free** in the subsequent conversion task, these 2,580 states need only be classified modulo the local group generated by \(H_0,H_1,S_0,S_1\). Exact partition yields **16 local Clifford orbits**, with respective sizes
\[
(36,24,72,72,144,72,144,144,144,144,144,288,576,144,288,144).
\]
Their sizes add to 2,580.

The [signed-orbit engine](../src/one_t_natural_input_theorem.py) constructs each gate's signed Pauli permutation from **Gaussian-integer 4×4 Clifford matrix numerators**. For Hadamards, the integer matrix \(H_{\rm num}=X+Z\) obeys \(H_{\rm num}PH_{\rm num}^\dagger=2(\pm Q)\); \(S\) and CNOT require no denominators. Thus orbit construction uses exact integer permutations and exact integer \((a,b)\) expectation coefficients, not floating-point matching.

## A complete constructive 16-class magic-saturating certificate

All Pauli expectations for these ≤2T inputs lie in \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\); the selected representatives have coefficients \(r_P=(a_P+b_P\sqrt2)/8\), with even integer \(a_P,b_P\). Re-express them as \((a_P/2+b_P\sqrt2/2)/4\), apply three \(T_{\pm P}\) rotations using exact integer-pair recurrences, and evaluate
\[
R=\frac{A+B\sqrt2}{32^4},\qquad A,B\in\mathbb Z.
\]

The following **chronological** Pauli-axis triples attain exactly \(R=7/4\) for the 16 representatives, all with **negative-axis signs**. Each (T_{-P}=T_P^\dagger) is first **mapped onto a weight-one Pauli axis** by the appropriate at-most-one-CNOT Clifford frame, then implemented using a standard T plus a local (S^\dagger) correction. For **weight-two Pauli axes**, the inverse-to-positive correction need not be local *before* conjugation; our physical one-CNOT compiler accounts for this explicitly:

| Local Clifford class | Signed axes (chronological, all negative) |
|---|---|
| 1 | IY, YI, YY |
| 2 | IX, IY, XY |
| 3 | IX, IY, ZY |
| 4 | XI, YI, YZ |
| 5 | IY, IX, ZX |
| 6 | IY, YX, ZZ |
| 7 | IY, ZI, YY |
| 8 | IZ, YI, YY |
| 9 | IY, ZY, YI |
| 10 | IY, ZY, YI |
| 11 | IX, IY, ZI |
| 12 | IX, IX, YI |
| 13 | XX, XX, XX |
| 14 | ZZ, ZX, ZX |
| 15 | IY, IY, IY |
| 16 | ZX, ZX, IY |

For every class the certificate asserts **the exact same integer result**
\[
\sum_{P}\langle P\rangle_{\rm out}^4=\frac{1\,835\,008}{1\,048\,576}=\frac74,
\quad B=0.
\]
It also verifies each triple's **physical one-CNOT compatibility** by enumerating local axis permutations and both CNOT orientations. Namely, it checks that the axes can be divided into a local prefix followed by a set conjugate to local axes under one CNOT plus local Cliffords. Negative signs do not add T gates **after each signed axis is mapped to a local physical T**: (T^\dagger=S^\dagger T) up to an overall phase on one qubit. Assuming the same local correction for a weight-two Pauli before localization would be incorrect.

A separate [direct complex-matrix test](../tests/test_two_t_prepared_no_go.py) reconstructs all 16 input density matrices from their exact Pauli vectors, extracts normalized pure states, directly applies the signed three-T rotations, and independently compiles each into a **0/1 physical CNOT** circuit. It checks the states agree up to a trailing Clifford, which preserves \(M_2\).

This is a **finite computer-assisted theorem**, not an abstract inequality valid for arbitrary multi-qubit systems. A separate implementation of the finite orbit classification, or a short hand-checkable invariant proof, would further strengthen it.

## Interpretation and next target

This is a stronger physical boundary than the previous engineered input witnesses:

- A magical state sourced from **one or two injected T gates** cannot gain additional final \(M_2\) by allowing a *second* CNOT, when the conversion is allowed up to three additional T gates. Indeed **one CNOT already reaches the absolute global maximum**.
- A strict two-CNOT input-conditioned advantage needs the supplied input to have **stabilizer-to-state T-count ≥3**.
- Whether a **three-T-prepared input** can already exhibit such a separation remains open; our known engineered witnesses have a preparation cost of four T.
- The result assumes **exact pure input states**, perfect Cliffords and T gates, ideal ancilla-free unitary circuits, free local Clifford gates, and that the source-state preparation cost is outside the conversion budget. No noisy-state or heralded-injection conclusion follows.

## Prior-art boundary

The maximum \(\log_2(16/7)\), maximally magical two-qubit states, and their Clifford orbit are known from Liu–Low–Yin (2025 preprint; 2026 journal) and Roman et al. (2026). The Pauli/Clifford normal form and symplectic group are also existing tools, including Bian–Selinger. Hou–Cao–Yang (2026) explicitly analyze magic injection involving nonstabilizer entanglement, though in a different ensemble/scope; Leone–Bittel (2024) establish stabilizer Rényi monotone conversion constraints. We have **not verified** whether the exact 2,580-state saturation theorem is already implicit in their detailed calculations or other literature. No publication-first claim is made.
